Case 005

$3M for Circumcisions in Zambia

The chart said $3 million. The real program cost $213 million — about seventy times more.

This case is on Senator Kennedy's July 15 list — watch him read it on the Senate floor.

  • The real program: USAID DISCOVER-Health, signed December 31, 2015 by USAID/Zambia and run by JSI Research & Training Institute — total obligations $213,003,798
  • It scaled up voluntary medical male circumcision and family planning across Zambia, provided HIV services, and socially marketed condoms and contraceptives
  • Circumcision stayed frozen under the February 1, 2025 treatment-only waiver, and the award was terminated on March 10, 2025

Who did us

The money (2015)

  • Barack Obama — President of the United States Signed the FY2016 funding law (P.L. 114-113) on December 18, 2015; its lump-sum Global Health Programs account is where the money came from.
  • Harold Rogers — Chairman, House Appropriations Committee Led the House committee that produced the funding law.
  • Nita Lowey — Ranking Member, House Appropriations Committee The committee's ranking member for the funding law.

The program (2015–2016)

  • Gayle E. Smith — Administrator, USAID Ran the agency when the DISCOVER-Health agreement was signed on December 31, 2015.
  • Deborah Birx — Global AIDS Coordinator (PEPFAR) Held the office that ran the AIDS program in 2015, when the award was made.
  • Michael Yates — Mission Director, USAID/Zambia In post as mission director when the award was signed.
  • Susan K. Brems — Mission Director, USAID/Zambia The mission director before him; her January 27, 2015 comments to the Inspector General describe DISCOVER as the designed follow-on to the expired PRISM activity.
  • Joel H. Lamstein — President & CEO, JSI Research & Training Institute Led the organization that received the award in 2015.

The program (final years)

  • Samantha Power — Administrator, USAID Ran the agency in the program's final years, from May 3, 2021 to January 20, 2025.
  • John N. Nkengasong — Global AIDS Coordinator (PEPFAR) Held the office from June 13, 2022 to January 20, 2025.
  • Peter Wiebler — Mission Director, USAID/Zambia Mission director in the program's final years, in post by August 2023 and still in the role at termination.
  • Margaret Crotty — President & CEO, JSI Research & Training Institute Led JSI in the program's final years, in the role from January 2022.
  • Muka Chikuba-McLeod — Project Director, USAID DISCOVER-Health Directed the project in Zambia, and was JSI's country representative there.
The chart said $3 million. The record says $213,003,798 in obligations on one award — and no bill ever named this item: Congress voted lump-sum global-health money, and USAID chose the program. One honest gap: no public document names the USAID officer who signed the December 31, 2015 agreement, so this page doesn't name one either.

Who's rescuing us

The 2025 figure and the end

  • Russell Vought — Director, Office of Management and Budget Wrote the April 2025 memo that flagged "$3 million for circumcision, vasectomies and condoms in Zambia" as an example of waste at USAID. He is still the budget director today.
  • John Kennedy — U.S. Senator, Louisiana Read the figure aloud on the Senate floor on July 15, 2025, from a chart of taxpayer spending.
  • Jodey Arrington — Chairman, House Budget Committee Published the same figure on June 13, 2025, under "PEPFAR Rescissions."
  • Donald Trump — President of the United States Sent Congress the 2025 rescissions package; his administration terminated the DISCOVER-Health award in March 2025.
  • Marco Rubio — Acting Administrator, USAID Announced the cancellation of 5,200 USAID awards on March 10, 2025 — the day this award's termination is dated.
