Case 003

$1M for Voter ID in Haiti

The chart said $1 million. The real agreement cost $6.8 million — and nobody in Congress ever voted on it.

This case is on Senator Kennedy's July 15 list — watch him read it on the Senate floor.

  • The real agreement: USAID's Haiti mission to the Organization of American States, signed May 31, 2023 — $2,344,940 at first, $6,819,764 in total obligations, $5,046,788.95 paid out
  • It paid Haiti's National Identification Office to issue the national ID card — the only document that allows a Haitian to vote
  • The money came from lump-sum foreign-aid accounts in the FY2023 funding law — nobody in Congress ever voted on this program, and the word "voter" appears in that law zero times

Who did us

The money (2022–2023)

  • Joe Biden — President of the United States Signed the FY2023 funding law (P.L. 117-328) on December 29, 2022. Its lump-sum foreign-aid accounts are where the money came from; the law never names this program.

The program (2023–2025)

  • Samantha Power — Administrator, USAID Ran the agency that made the award, and was in office when it was signed on May 31, 2023.
  • Jennifer Link — Mission Director, USAID/Haiti Her mission issued the agreement with the OAS.
  • Luis Almagro — Secretary General, Organization of American States Led the organization that received the award and fronted the project publicly: "This project began with a contribution from USAID."
  • Katie Taylor — Executive Director, Pan American Development Foundation (PADF) Ran the OAS's partner foundation, named with the OAS in the agreement itself — "OAS/PADF."
  • Reynold Guerrier — Director General, Haiti National Identification Office (ONI) Ran the office that registered citizens and issued the ID cards, for the program's entire life.

The votes

  • FY2023 funding law (P.L. 117-328, H.R. 2617): Senate 68–29 (Dec 22, 2022, Vote 421); House 225–201 (Dec 23, 2022, Roll Call 549) — a vote on lump-sum accounts, not on this program, which no bill ever named
The chart said $1 million. The record says $6,819,764. One honest gap: the USAID officer who signed the agreement is named in no public document, so this page doesn't name one either. The Trump administration terminated the U.S. funding on March 1, 2025; Japan picked the project up, and over 1.22 million ID cards have been delivered.

Who's rescuing us

  • Russell Vought — Director, Office of Management and Budget Wrote the April 2025 memo that flagged this spending.
  • Jodey Arrington — Chairman, House Budget Committee Published the figure on his rescissions list in June 2025.
  • John Kennedy — U.S. Senator, Louisiana Read it aloud on the Senate floor on July 15, 2025, from his chart of taxpayer spending.
  • Donald Trump — President of the United States His administration terminated the U.S. funding on March 1, 2025, and he sent Congress the rescissions package.
