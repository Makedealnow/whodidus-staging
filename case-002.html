Case 002

$1.1B for Public Broadcasting

The exception. Congress voted on this money by name — twice — and then voted to take it back.

This case is on Senator Kennedy's July 15 list — watch him read it on the Senate floor.

  • $535 million for 2026 and $535 million for 2027, both written into law by name for the Corporation for Public Broadcasting
  • The 2025 Rescissions Act cancelled both amounts, also by name
  • The president who signed the second funding law is the president who signed the cancellation

Who did us

  • Joe Biden — President of the United States Signed the law that funded public broadcasting for 2026 ($535 million) on March 23, 2024.
  • Susan Collins — U.S. Senator, Maine; Chair, Senate Appropriations Committee Voted no on the rescission.

Who put the CPB money in

  • Tammy Baldwin — U.S. Senator, Wisconsin; Senate Labor-HHS chair (118th Congress) The $535M came from her subcommittee's Senate bill (S. 2624).
  • Shelley Moore Capito — U.S. Senator, West Virginia; Senate Labor-HHS ranking member Her own March 21, 2024 press release highlighted the $535M for CPB.
  • Rosa DeLauro — U.S. Representative, Connecticut; House Labor-HHS ranking member
  • Kay Granger — U.S. Representative, Texas; House Appropriations chair Chair at enactment of P.L. 118-47, the first funding law.
  • Tom Cole — U.S. Representative, Oklahoma; House Appropriations chair Succeeded Granger, and later sponsored H.R. 1968 (P.L. 119-4), the continuing resolution that carried the FY2027 $535M forward.
  • Patty Murray — U.S. Senator, Washington; Senate Appropriations chair (118th Congress) Chaired the committee for the first funding law; the chair and vice-chair roles flipped with Collins in the 119th Congress.
  • Susan Collins — U.S. Senator, Maine; Senate Appropriations vice chair (118th Congress) Vice chair for the first funding law; chair in the 119th Congress.

Who championed it

  • Earl Blumenauer — U.S. Representative, Oregon; founder/co-chair, Public Broadcasting Caucus Defended CPB funding on the floor (Feb 28, 2024). Co-chairs: Mark Amodei (R-NV), Dan Goldman (D-NY), Jake Ellzey (R-TX).

Who tried to save it in 2025

  • Tammy Baldwin — U.S. Senator, Wisconsin Offered the amendment to strike the CPB rescission: rejected 47–52 (July 16, 2025).
  • Lisa Murkowski & Susan Collins — U.S. Senators, Alaska and Maine Offered a narrower amendment: rejected 47–51.

Who's rescuing us

  • Donald Trump — President of the United States Signed the law that funded public broadcasting for 2027 ($535 million) in March 2025 — then signed the Rescissions Act cancelling both amounts on July 24, 2025.
  • Russell Vought — Director, Office of Management and Budget His office wrote the rescission proposals that named public broadcasting.
  • Steve Scalise — U.S. Representative, Louisiana; House Majority Leader Introduced the Rescissions Act (H.R. 4) in the House.
  • John Kennedy — U.S. Senator, Louisiana Put the $1.1 billion on his Senate floor chart on July 15, 2025, and voted yes on the rescission.
  • Eric Schmitt — U.S. Senator, Missouri Offered the substitute amendment that set the final Senate version of the bill.
  • Robert Aderholt — U.S. Representative, Alabama; House Labor-HHS chair His House bill (H.R. 5894) gave CPB zero. It never passed.

The votes

  • First funding law (P.L. 118-47, H.R. 2882): House 286–134 (Mar 22, 2024, Roll 102); Senate 74–24 (Mar 23, 2024, Vote 114)
  • Second funding law (P.L. 119-4, H.R. 1968): House 217–213 (Mar 11, 2025, Roll 70); Senate 54–46 (Mar 14, 2025, Vote 133)
  • Rescissions Act, Senate passage, July 17, 2025: 51–48 — every yes vote was a Republican; Senators Collins and Murkowski voted no
  • Rescissions Act, House agrees to the Senate version, July 18, 2025: 216–213
  • Rescissions Act, House first passage, June 12, 2025: 214–212
The House bill cut it to zero. The Senate put it back. Both presidents signed it. — 457 of the members who voted yes on those two funding laws are still in Congress, too many to list here; this page names the writers, champions, and signers, and the documents below link the official roll calls.
