Case 008

$4.4M for the Melanesian Youth Climate Corps

USAID's own announcement said $4.4 million — for a youth climate corps across four Pacific island nations.

This case is on Senator Kennedy's July 15 list — watch him read it on the Senate floor.

  • The real award: Cooperative Agreement 720BHA24CA00005, signed March 22, 2024 by USAID's Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance — $4,446,854 over four years to Social Solutions International, Inc.
  • It was competed under a 2022 agency program statement and paid from lump-sum foreign aid accounts — it was never a line in any bill, and nobody in Congress ever voted on it
  • The Rescissions Act did not kill it — its money was already obligated. It died in the executive branch's 2025 shutdown of USAID

Who did us

The program (2024)

  • Samantha Power — Administrator, USAID Ran the agency for the entire award period until January 20, 2025.
  • Sonali Korde — Assistant to the Administrator, Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance, USAID Sworn in on February 12, 2024 — five weeks before her bureau signed the award — and publicly touted the $4.4 million corps in a May 17, 2024 keynote at the Center for Strategic and International Studies.
  • Sarah Charles — Assistant to the Administrator, Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance, USAID (2021 – February 2024) Ran the bureau when the 2022 Climate Smart and Disaster Ready competition that produced this award was issued.
  • Zema Semunegus — Mission Director, USAID Pacific Islands Inaugurated as the new mission's first director at its opening in Suva in August 2023 — the mission for the place of performance.

Who received it

  • Susanna Nemes — CEO and Founder, Social Solutions International, Inc. Led the Maryland firm that received Cooperative Agreement 720BHA24CA00005.
  • Jenny Namur Karp — Co-Founder and President, Social Solutions International, Inc. Co-founded and led the firm that received the award.
  • Martin Child — Director, Climate Smart & Disaster Ready Program, Suva Directed the program in the field from May 2024, under which the Melanesian Youth Climate Corps ran.

The money (2022)

  • Barbara Lee — U.S. Representative, California; Chair, House State/Foreign Operations Subcommittee (117th Congress) Chaired the subcommittee that wrote the lump-sum foreign aid law in force when the award was competed.
  • Chris Coons — U.S. Senator, Delaware; Chair, Senate State/Foreign Operations Subcommittee (117th Congress) Chaired the Senate subcommittee that wrote the same lump-sum law.
  • Hal Rogers — U.S. Representative, Kentucky; Ranking Member, House State/Foreign Operations Subcommittee The subcommittee's ranking member for the funding law.
  • Joe Biden — President of the United States Signed the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023 (P.L. 117-328) on December 29, 2022; its Division K carried the lump-sum accounts this award was paid from.
The chart said $4.4 million — USAID's own rounded announcement number. The record says $4,446,854 on one cooperative agreement, chosen by agency officials out of lump sums Congress voted in bulk. One honest gap: no public document names the USAID Agreement Officer who signed the award, so this page doesn't name one either.

Who's rescuing us

  • John Kennedy — U.S. Senator, Louisiana Put the $4.4 million on his Senate floor chart of taxpayer spending in July 2025.
  • Donald Trump — President of the United States His administration terminated Social Solutions International's USAID contracts in the 2025 shutdown of USAID — ending this program in March 2025, a year into its four-year run.
