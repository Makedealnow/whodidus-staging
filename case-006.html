Case 006

$500K for Electric Buses in Rwanda

The chart said $500,000. The real grant was $1.5 million — three times more.

This case is on Senator Kennedy's July 15 list — watch him read it on the Senate floor.

  • The real award: USAID Grant 7200AA23FA00017 to BasiGo, Inc., signed September 13, 2023 through the Development Innovation Ventures program — $1,500,000 to put electric buses into service with Kigali's largest bus operators
  • Only $525,000 of the $1.5 million was ever paid out before the money stopped
  • The grant was stop-worked in January 2025 and listed in DOGE's terminated-grants data on March 1, 2025

Who did us

The money (2022–2023)

  • Joe Biden — President of the United States Signed the FY2023 funding law (P.L. 117-328) on December 29, 2022. Its lump-sum Development Assistance account is where the money came from; the law never names this grant.
  • Chris Coons — U.S. Senator, Delaware; Chair, Senate State & Foreign Operations Subcommittee Wrote the Senate foreign-aid bill that carried the money.
  • Lindsey Graham — U.S. Senator, South Carolina; Ranking Member, Senate State & Foreign Operations Subcommittee
  • Barbara Lee — U.S. Representative, California; Chair, House State & Foreign Operations Subcommittee Wrote the House foreign-aid bill that carried the money.
  • Hal Rogers — U.S. Representative, Kentucky; Ranking Member, House State & Foreign Operations Subcommittee

The program (2023–2025)

  • Samantha Power — Administrator, USAID Ran the agency when the BasiGo grant was signed on September 13, 2023.
  • Sasha Gallant — Chief, Development Innovation Ventures (DIV), USAID Ran the USAID program that selected and made the award.
  • Jonathan Kamin — Mission Director, USAID/Rwanda Ran USAID's mission in Rwanda through the award period; the grant itself was issued from Washington under DIV.
  • Jit Bhattacharya — Chief Executive Officer & Co-founder, BasiGo, Inc. Led the company that received the grant, and fronted its announcement: "We are grateful for the financial support of USAID in accelerating our public transport electrification plan in Rwanda."
  • Jonathan Green — Co-founder, BasiGo, Inc. Co-founded the company that received the grant.
  • Doreen Orishaba — Managing Director, BasiGo Rwanda Ran BasiGo's operation in Rwanda, quoted running it in March 2025.
The chart said $500,000. The record says one grant of $1,500,000 — and no bill ever named this item: Congress voted lump-sum development money, the FY2023 explanatory statement directed $40 million to the DIV program, and USAID chose BasiGo. One honest gap: no public document names the USAID officer who signed the grant, so this page doesn't name one either.

Who's rescuing us

The 2025 figure and the end

  • Russell Vought — Director, Office of Management and Budget Wrote the April 2025 memo that first flagged "$500,000 for electric buses in Rwanda" as an example of waste at USAID. He is still the budget director today.
  • Jodey Arrington — Chairman, House Budget Committee Published the same figure on June 13, 2025, in his release on the rescissions package.
  • John Kennedy — U.S. Senator, Louisiana Put the item on his Senate-floor chart of taxpayer spending and read it aloud on July 15, 2025.
  • Donald Trump — President of the United States Sent Congress the 2025 rescissions package; his administration stop-worked the grant in January 2025, and it was listed as terminated on March 1, 2025.
