Case 010

$3.6M for Pastry Classes in Haiti

The chart said $3.6 million. The one real program that matches the description carries $19.6 million in obligations — more than five times the chart figure.

This case is on Senator Kennedy's July 15 list — watch him read it on the Senate floor.

  • The real program: USAID's "Epidemic Control among Priority Populations," a PEPFAR award to Fondation SEROvie in Haiti — total obligations $19,648,794, February 2020 to February 2028 as amended
  • Its drop-in centers offered HIV services, plus psychosocial counseling and vocational training — the documented training includes cooking and computer skills
  • The award was listed for termination in 2025, but the latest data shows it still implementing under the PEPFAR waiver

Who did us

The money (2020)

  • Nita Lowey — Chairwoman, House State & Foreign Operations Subcommittee Wrote the House foreign operations bill that carried the Global Health Programs money.
  • Hal Rogers — Ranking Member, House State & Foreign Operations Subcommittee The subcommittee's ranking member for the FY2020 foreign operations bill.
  • Patrick Leahy — Ranking Member, Senate State & Foreign Operations Subcommittee The subcommittee's ranking member for the FY2020 foreign operations bill.

The program (2020–2025)

  • Mark Green — Administrator, USAID Ran the agency when the SEROvie award was made in February 2020.
  • Gary Juste — Acting Mission Director, USAID/Haiti Acting mission director when the award began on February 3, 2020.
  • Jennifer Link — Mission Director, USAID/Haiti Ran the Haiti mission from 2022 to 2025, during the program's largest audited years; the Inspector General's audit reports for those years are addressed to her.
  • Reginald Dupont — Executive Director, Fondation SEROvie Led the organization that received the award during the program years.
  • Steeve Laguerre — Founder, Fondation SEROvie Founded SEROvie in 1999 and led it in its earlier years.
The money came from the lump-sum Global Health Programs account in the FY2020 funding law (P.L. 116-94) — $9.1 billion for the account, including $4.7 billion for bilateral HIV/AIDS work under PEPFAR. The law passed the House 297–120 and the Senate 71–23. No bill ever named this program, and no public document names the USAID officer who signed the award — so this page doesn't name one either. Audited spending on the award ran $3,494,906 in FY2021, $3,504,008 in FY2022, and $4,639,029 in FY2023.

Who's rescuing us

The 2025 figure and the cuts

  • Russell Vought — Director, Office of Management and Budget Flagged this spending in his prepared testimony to the Senate Appropriations Committee on June 25, 2025. He is still the budget director today.
  • John Kennedy — U.S. Senator, Louisiana Read the figure aloud on the Senate floor on July 15, 2025, from a chart of taxpayer spending.
  • Donald Trump — President of the United States Sent Congress the rescissions package proposing to claw back PEPFAR money.
